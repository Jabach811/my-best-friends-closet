Joel only · do not hand over

Seven minutes, then out.

Late day, not at close
Get a day — not a decision

The seven minutes

0:00
Names first, before anything else. "Hi — are you Liz? I'm Joel." If there's a customer at the counter, browse the racks until they're free. Never pitch over a paying customer.
0:20
Tell them you're not selling. This is the line that relaxes them. "I live here in Tracy. I build websites for small shops. I'm not here to sell you anything — I'm dropping something off and getting out of your hair."
0:45
The reveal. One sentence, then the phone goes in their hands. "I kept thinking your website wasn't doing this place justice. So I rebuilt it. It's live right now."
1:15
Now say nothing. Count to thirty. Hardest part of the visit. Don't narrate, don't point, don't explain. Whatever they say first tells you everything you need.
2:30
Scroll them to the Instagram strip. This is the peak — their own photos on their own new site. "That's your Instagram, pulling in by itself. Eleven thousand followers, three live sales a week — and your site right now doesn't mention a word of it."
3:30
Take the phone back. Say the no-risk line once and stop. "Nothing changes until you say so. Your site stays right where it is. You'd see this done and live before you paid me anything. And the price will fit a shop this size."
4:30
Hand over the sheet. Point at exactly two things. The square — "scan that tonight, it'll pull up on your own phones." Then the bottom block — "that part's the fun one. Anything you two dream up, I'll build it."
5:30
Ask for a day, not a decision. Then shut up and let them answer. "I'm not asking you to decide anything tonight. Can I call you Friday?"
6:30
Leave. Even if it's going great — especially if it's going great. "I gotta get this guy home before he turns." Smile, out the door. No lingering goodbye.

Do not

  • Don't give a tour.You're not walking them through the site. They'll explore it tonight — that's the whole plan.
  • Don't lead with what's broken.It's on the sheet in writing. Let the paper be the bad guy while you stay the nice one.
  • Don't say a number.Not even a range. Not even if they push once.
  • Don't work the kid.He helps because he's just there — a neighbor with his son, not a salesman. The second you're using him, they'll feel it.
  • Don't stay past seven.Leave while they still want more. That hunger is what carries them all night.

If they ask

  • "How much is this?""Honestly don't know yet — depends what you want me handling. Tell me that and I'll put it in writing this week."
  • "We need to think about it.""That's exactly what I want you to do. Can I call Friday?"
  • "We get all our business from Instagram.""That's the thing — Instagram's doing all the work and the website drops the handoff."
  • "Who are you, again?""I live here. My cell's on that sheet — call me anytime. And everything I build ends up belonging to you."

Only if they ask what's wrong with theirs

  • Sixteen seconds to open on a phone.Most people give it three.
  • Last on page one for "consignment shop Tracy."Under Yelp — for their own name.
  • The consignment page never asks for the sale.No hours, no address, no way to book a drop-off.

Say one. Not all three. Then get off it — you're not there to make them feel bad about the shop they built.

Before you walk in

  • Scan the QR on the printed sheet yourselfBefore you print the rest of them.
  • New site already loaded and sitting on the screenPhone unlocked, brightness up, notifications off. No typing in front of them.
  • Both sheets out and in your handNot in a bag you have to dig through with a stroller.
  • Go late, but not at closeThirty to forty-five minutes before they lock up. Not five.
  • Know your number anywayNot to say — so you don't freeze if they ask point blank.

Swoop in, hand them something exciting, leave before the excitement peaks.
