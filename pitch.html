What the current site is costing you — starting with the phone

On a phone, your shop takes sixteen seconds to appear.
Sixteen seconds of blank screen before a single photo of your racks shows up. Most people give up after about three — they tap back and land on Yelp, next to a list of other consignment stores. The new site shows the shop immediately.
Then it's twelve full screens of scrolling to reach the bottom.
I measured it on a normal phone: your homepage is about twelve screens tall, with a dozen buttons too small to tap cleanly and text too small to read without pinching. The new homepage says the same things in about seven, at a size you can actually read.
In all twelve of those screens, there is no address, no hours, and no phone number.
That's the one thing a woman on her phone is looking for. Some give up hunting; some drive downtown on a Monday and find the door locked. The new site puts your address, hours, and number on every screen — one tap to call, one tap for directions.
Shoppers see four items, then hit a dead end.
Browsing the racks is the whole joy of your shop. The new site puts all 222 in-stock pieces online — real prices, real photos — with the sort buttons pinned to the top of the screen so they stay in reach while you scroll. Checkout still runs through your existing store.
Googling “consignment shop Tracy” finds Yelp before it finds you.
Your own site comes in last on the page, partly because it never says the word “consignment” in its title and doesn't publish your address and hours in the format Google reads. The new site does both.
